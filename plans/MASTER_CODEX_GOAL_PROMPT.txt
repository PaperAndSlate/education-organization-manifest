Core outcome:
Create an internationally usable, school-focused first release of a neutral protocol that lets educational organizations publish public, machine-readable institutional information through one well-known manifest. The manifest establishes identity, scope, authority, capabilities, linked resources, delegation, versioning, provenance, and optional integrity/signature metadata.

The root manifest must stay compact. Rich data belongs in optional linked modules.

Required v1 modules:
organization profiles, campuses, departments, staff directory, contacts, courses, offerings and sections, programs and pathways, calendars, events, facilities, services, policies/documents, admissions/enrollment information, sports, transportation, meals/menus, clubs, jobs, news, public aggregate statistics, and API/service discovery.

Non-negotiable safety boundary:
Never model or include student-level data, grades, individual attendance, IEP/504/SEN/medical/safeguarding records, discipline, private schedules, private transportation assignments, secrets, credentials, or internal-only endpoints. Staff data is allowed only when deliberately public. Prefer role contacts.

Authority and delegation:
- HTTPS origin is root authority.
- Source ownership and publication authority are separate.
- Support CODEOWNERS and modular source ownership.
- Support explicit cross-origin/vendor resource delegation.
- No transitive delegation by default.
- Signatures are optional in v1.
- A future paper&slate index is a convenience observer, not the authority.

Internationalization:
- no U.S.-only core assumptions;
- BCP 47 language tags;
- multilingual values and text direction;
- jurisdiction profiles for grades, education levels, credits, governance, and identifiers.

Course modeling:
- deeply model optional course metadata;
- distinguish course definitions from offerings and sections;
- isolate live availability/enrollment information in an optional layer;
- support prerequisites, credits, outcomes, standards, topics, modes, pathways, fees, materials, qualifications, effective dates, and related courses.

Provenance:
- resource-, object-, and field-level provenance;
- source URI, source type, observation time, effective period, license, transformation, verification status, and confidence where applicable;
- preserve conflicts instead of silently overwriting;
- implement the approved precedence model.

Agentic generation:
- agents may extract from public websites, approved documents, catalogs, handbooks, calendars, government records, public exports, and questionnaires;
- agents must create evidence-led candidates and pull requests;
- no direct publication by default;
- keep confidence and evidence metadata in an internal review workspace;
- add privacy, stale-data, and source-verification review gates.
