# EOM 1.0 Ownership and Delegation

Source ownership controls edits to authoring files; publication authority controls which party may publish an authoritative resource. They are separate. The origin serving the root is the authority anchor. A root delegation names a delegate, resource type/ID, allowed origin/path, validity period, status, optional key scope, and subject. `validFrom` and `validUntil` are required finite date-times with `validFrom < validUntil`; the evaluation time must be inside that interval and before any `revokedAt` value. When `keys` is present it is a non-empty allowlist of signing key identifiers. Consumers verify presence, resource/type/ID scope, final URL origin/path, time, status, keys, and subject before accepting a delegated resource.

Cross-origin linked resources without signatures are valid when explicitly root-linked, but UIs must label publisher, maintainer, delegate, and signer separately. The subject set declared by a resource descriptor must equal the subject context published by the fetched document; an extra observed subject is not authorized. `transitive: true` is rejected by stable v1 tooling unless an explicit experimental profile is enabled. Authority is evaluated against the observed final URL, not only the declared URL; every redirect and cache hit is rechecked. A root discovery redirect may not cross origins before a trusted root manifest exists. Revocation, expiry, vendor change, historical evidence, and module failure are preserved; a vendor does not become the school identity.
